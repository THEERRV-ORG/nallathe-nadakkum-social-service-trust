1. Purpose

Nallathe Nadakkum Samuga Sevai Arakkattalai ("the Trust") works with people in acute distress, and our work brings us into contact with children — through education support, family assistance, emergency medical cases, food distribution and community outreach.

This Policy sets out how the Trust protects children from harm, what every person acting for the Trust must and must not do, and exactly what to do if a concern arises. It exists to make the right action obvious at the moment it matters, when someone is uncertain and under pressure.

It is written in plain language on purpose. A safeguarding policy that people cannot understand does not protect anyone.

2. Our Commitment

The Trust believes every child has the right to be safe, to be treated with dignity, and to be protected from all forms of abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence — regardless of caste, religion, gender, disability, family circumstance or economic status.

We commit to:

  • place the child's safety and best interests above all other considerations, including the reputation of the Trust, the convenience of an activity, or the standing of any individual;
  • take every concern seriously — no concern about a child is too small to raise;
  • never conceal, minimise or internally "settle" an allegation of harm to a child;
  • comply fully with our reporting obligations under Indian law;
  • maintain an environment in which children, families, volunteers and the public feel safe to speak up;
  • protect anyone who reports a concern in good faith from retaliation.

We recognise that abuse of children is most often committed by someone the child knows and trusts. This Policy therefore applies to our own people first, and does not assume good character on the basis of position, seniority or reputation.

3. Scope — Who This Applies To

This Policy applies, without exception, to:

  • Trustees, including the Chairman and Treasurer;
  • Staff, whether paid, part-time or honorary;
  • Volunteers, including occasional and one-day volunteers;
  • Interns and students placed with the Trust;
  • Contractors, drivers and service providers engaged by the Trust;
  • Donors, sponsors, media and visitors attending Trust activities or field sites;
  • Partner organisations delivering activities jointly with us.

It applies at our office, at distribution points, in hospitals and government institutions, during home and school visits, in vehicles including the ambulance, at events, and online.

Everyone within scope must read this Policy and confirm in writing that they accept it before beginning any activity that may bring them into contact with children.

4. Definitions

  • Child — any person who has not completed eighteen (18) years of age, in line with the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015 and the POCSO Act, 2012. Where age is uncertain, the person is treated as a child until established otherwise.
  • Child abuse — any act or failure to act that harms or threatens a child's health, survival, development or dignity. It includes physical, sexual and emotional abuse, neglect, and exploitation.
  • Safeguarding — the steps we take in advance to prevent harm, not merely our response after harm occurs.
  • Child Protection Officer (CPO) — the person designated under Section 13 to receive concerns and coordinate the Trust's response.
  • Reporter — any person who raises a concern under this Policy.

5. Legal Framework

This Policy is framed under, and does not displace, the following laws:

  • The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012 (as amended in 2019) — defines sexual offences against children and imposes a mandatory duty to report. See Section 14.
  • The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 — governs children in need of care and protection, and creates offences of cruelty, employment for begging, and giving intoxicants to a child.
  • The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 — general criminal law, including offences of assault, kidnapping, trafficking and cruelty.
  • The Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986 (as amended in 2016) — prohibits employment of children below 14 years and of adolescents in hazardous occupations.
  • The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006.
  • The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 — including the prohibition on physical punishment and mental harassment.
  • The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — requires verifiable parental consent before processing a child's personal data, and prohibits tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children.
  • The Information Technology Act, 2000 — including Section 67B, which criminalises publishing or transmitting material depicting children in sexually explicit acts.

Where this Policy and the law differ, the law prevails. Where this Policy sets a higher standard than the law requires, this Policy applies.

6. Guiding Principles

  1. Zero tolerance. The Trust does not tolerate any form of abuse, exploitation or neglect of a child by any person associated with it.
  2. The child comes first. Where the interests of a child conflict with the interests of the Trust, an individual, a donor or a programme, the child's interests prevail.
  3. Prevention over reaction. We design activities to reduce risk before it arises.
  4. Believe and act. A child who discloses harm is to be believed and taken seriously. It is not for us to decide whether an allegation is true — that is for the authorities.
  5. Do no further harm. Our response must not expose the child to further distress, stigma or risk.
  6. Accountability. Every person within scope is personally responsible for compliance. Not knowing the Policy is not a defence.

7. Code of Conduct

7.1 You must

  • treat every child with respect, patience and dignity;
  • be visible — remain where you can be seen by other adults when with a child;
  • listen to children and take what they say seriously;
  • report any concern immediately, however small or uncertain;
  • obtain parental or guardian consent before any interaction, photograph or record involving a child;
  • respect a child's privacy and personal boundaries;
  • challenge, or report, behaviour by others that breaches this Code.

7.2 You must never

  • be alone with a child where you cannot be observed, including in a closed room, a vehicle, or an isolated place;
  • use physical punishment, or any form of corporal, humiliating or degrading discipline;
  • shout at, threaten, mock, shame or verbally abuse a child;
  • engage in any sexual activity, contact, conversation, gesture or innuendo with a child — a child's apparent consent is irrelevant and is no defence in law;
  • touch a child in a way that is, or could be interpreted as, inappropriate;
  • develop a private relationship with a child met through the Trust's work, or contact them privately;
  • give a child money, gifts or favours outside an approved programme, or seek any favour in return for assistance;
  • invite a child to your home, or visit a child's home alone;
  • transport a child alone in a vehicle, except in a genuine emergency — which must be reported to the CPO immediately afterwards;
  • consume alcohol, tobacco or any intoxicant in the presence of a child, or attend an activity under the influence;
  • share a child's name, photograph, school, address or location publicly or on personal social media;
  • employ a child, or place a child in work of any kind;
  • discriminate against a child on any ground.

Behaviour that is well-intentioned can still be unsafe. Giving a struggling student money directly, offering a lift home, or keeping in touch privately may all come from kindness — but they place both the child and you at risk, and they bypass the safeguards that protect everyone. Route all support through the Trust.

8. Contact With Children

8.1 Home and school visits

Verification visits for education support bring volunteers into a child's home and school. These carry the highest risk in our work and are governed by strict rules:

  • Visits are conducted by at least two authorised persons. Solo visits to a child's home are not permitted.
  • A parent, guardian or responsible adult must be present throughout.
  • Visits take place during daylight hours and are notified in advance where possible.
  • Volunteers carry identification issued by the Trust and state clearly who they are and why they have come.
  • School visits require the knowledge and permission of the school authority.
  • Questions are limited to what the verification requires. Volunteers do not interview a child alone or probe into personal or family matters beyond the purpose of the visit.
  • A brief written record of each visit is made and filed with the Trust.

8.2 Distribution, events and field activity

  • Children present at food distribution or events remain the responsibility of their accompanying adult.
  • Volunteers do not separate a child from their family or take a child anywhere.
  • Where an unaccompanied child is found in distress, volunteers must not take the child into their own care. Contact 1098 or the police immediately, remain with the child in a public place until help arrives, and inform the CPO.

8.3 Ambulance and hospital settings

  • A child transported in the Trust's ambulance must be accompanied by a parent, guardian or hospital staff member wherever possible.
  • Where an emergency makes this impossible, at least two Trust personnel must be present, and the journey must be reported to the CPO on the same day.
  • Trust personnel do not undertake medical procedures on a child.

9. Recruitment and Screening

Safeguarding begins at the point a person joins us.

  • Every volunteer, staff member and intern provides proof of identity and address and at least one reference before engagement.
  • Everyone signs a written declaration confirming they have read this Policy, accept the Code of Conduct, and have never been convicted of, charged with, or dismissed or asked to resign in connection with, an offence or allegation involving a child.
  • Persons whose role involves direct or unsupervised contact with children — including education support verification visits — are subject to enhanced checks, including police verification.
  • New volunteers work under supervision and do not have unsupervised contact with children until the Trust is satisfied as to their suitability.
  • Any person who declines to provide the declaration or required checks will not be engaged in work involving children.
  • A false declaration is grounds for immediate removal and may be reported to the police.

10. Photographs, Images and Stories

Photographs of children are the most common way a well-meaning charity causes harm. The following rules are absolute.

  • Verifiable written consent of a parent or lawful guardian is obtained before any photograph, video or recording of a child, and separately before publication. Consent is explained in a language the family understands.
  • The child's own agreement is sought where they are old enough to understand, and a child's refusal is respected even where a guardian consents.
  • We never publish a child's full name, school name, precise address or location, or any combination of details that would allow the child to be found.
  • Children are photographed appropriately dressed, never in a state of undress, distress or medical exposure.
  • Images must be truthful and not staged, captioned misleadingly, or used to exaggerate hardship for fundraising effect.
  • Images of children must not be used to depict a situation the child was not part of.
  • Photographs are taken on Trust-authorised devices where possible, transferred promptly to Trust storage, and deleted from personal devices. Volunteers do not keep images of children.
  • Consent may be withdrawn at any time. On request we remove the material from our website and social media within a reasonable period.
  • Donors, media and visitors may not photograph children at Trust activities without the express permission of the CPO and the guardian.
  • Where a child is a beneficiary of last-rites, medical or destitution assistance, we exercise additional restraint and generally do not publish identifiable images at all.

11. Digital and Online Safety

  • Communication with a child is through a parent or guardian, not directly and privately.
  • Personnel must not add, follow or privately message a child on social media, or exchange personal phone numbers with a child.
  • Any WhatsApp or messaging group involving beneficiaries must include a guardian and at least two Trust personnel.
  • In line with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, the Trust does not track, monitor or profile children, and does not direct behavioural or targeted advertising at children.
  • Any material depicting a child in a sexual manner must be reported immediately and never forwarded, saved or shared — forwarding such material is itself an offence under Section 67B of the Information Technology Act, 2000.

12. Recognising Harm

You are not expected to investigate or diagnose. You are expected to notice and report. Indicators may include:

  • Physical — unexplained injuries, bruises or burns; injuries inconsistent with the explanation given; fear of a particular adult; flinching from contact.
  • Sexual — sexual knowledge or behaviour inappropriate to age; discomfort walking or sitting; withdrawal; disclosure, direct or indirect.
  • Emotional — persistent fear, anxiety or withdrawal; extreme passivity or aggression; self-harm; being consistently belittled or ignored.
  • Neglect — persistent hunger, poor hygiene, untreated illness, inadequate clothing, being left unsupervised or out of school.
  • Exploitation — working during school hours, begging, unexplained money or gifts, being controlled by an adult outside the family.

Poverty is not neglect. Many families we serve are in acute hardship without any fault or failure of care. Judgement must be exercised carefully and without stigma — but where you are unsure, report. It is not your responsibility to be certain.

13.2 What to do

  1. Ensure the child is safe. If there is immediate danger, call 1098 or 112 first.
  2. Do not investigate. Do not question the child in detail, seek proof, or confront the person concerned. Investigation is for the authorities, and a well-meant enquiry can compromise a case.
  3. Listen if a child discloses. Stay calm, do not react with shock or disgust, do not promise secrecy, and tell the child that they were right to speak and that you must tell someone who can help.
  4. Record what you observed as soon as possible — date, time, place, who was present, and what was said, in the child's own words as far as you can recall. Do not add interpretation.
  5. Report to the CPO the same day. By phone if urgent, followed in writing.
  6. Tell no one else. Do not discuss the matter with colleagues, family, the community or on social media.

You must report even if you are unsure, even if you have no proof, and even if the person concerned is senior, respected, or a trustee. Reporting in good faith carries no consequence for you, even if the concern turns out to be unfounded. Failing to report does.

13.3 Concerns from outside the Trust

Families, beneficiaries, schools, partners and members of the public may raise a concern using the contact details above. Concerns may be raised anonymously; we will act on them so far as the information allows.

14. Mandatory Reporting Under POCSO

Reporting a sexual offence against a child is a legal duty, not a choice.

Under Section 19 of the POCSO Act, 2012, any person who apprehends that a sexual offence against a child is likely to be committed, or knows that one has been committed, must report it to the Special Juvenile Police Unit or the local police.

Under Section 21, failing to report is itself a punishable offence. This duty binds every trustee, staff member and volunteer personally. It cannot be delegated, deferred, or overridden by any instruction from within the Trust.

Accordingly:

  • A POCSO concern is reported to the police without delay, and in any event within twenty-four (24) hours of the Trust becoming aware.
  • The Trust will not conduct an internal enquiry before reporting, and will never treat internal review as a substitute for reporting.
  • No person may be instructed, pressured or persuaded not to report. Any such instruction is void and is itself a serious breach of this Policy.
  • The report is made regardless of the identity or position of the person concerned, and regardless of the effect on the Trust's reputation, funding or programmes.
  • Where the child or family is reluctant, the duty to report still applies. We support the family through the process, but we do not withhold the report.
  • The Trust will cooperate fully with the police, the Child Welfare Committee and any Special Court.

Reports may also be made through the POCSO e-Box at pocsoebox.gov.in, an online facility of the National Commission for Protection of Child Rights.

15. Responding to a Report

On receiving a concern, the Child Protection Officer will:

  1. Secure the child's immediate safety and welfare as the first step.
  2. Record the concern in the Trust's confidential safeguarding register.
  3. Assess whether a mandatory report is required and, if so, make it within the timeline in Section 14 — before any internal process.
  4. Inform the Board of Trustees, on a strictly need-to-know basis.
  5. Suspend the person concerned from all contact with children, as a neutral precautionary measure pending the outcome. Suspension is not a finding of guilt.
  6. Notify the parent or guardian, unless doing so would place the child at greater risk — for example, where the concern relates to a family member.
  7. Cooperate fully with the police, the Child Welfare Committee and any other authority.
  8. Review what happened once the matter concludes, and change our practice where a weakness is identified.

The Trust will not offer, accept or facilitate any settlement, compromise or compensation to prevent or withdraw a report to the authorities.

16. Supporting the Child

Throughout and after any concern, the Trust will:

  • keep the child informed in a way appropriate to their age and understanding;
  • respect the child's dignity, privacy and identity, and never disclose their identity publicly — publication of a child victim's identity is prohibited under Section 23 of the POCSO Act, 2012;
  • help the family access medical care, counselling and legal aid, including through the District Child Protection Unit and District Legal Services Authority;
  • continue the child's assistance from the Trust wherever possible, so that reporting never costs a family the support they depend on;
  • ensure the child is not blamed, isolated or made to feel responsible.

17. Confidentiality and Records

  • Safeguarding information is shared strictly on a need-to-know basis and only with those who must act on it.
  • Records are kept securely, with access restricted to the CPO and the Board.
  • Children's personal data is handled in accordance with our Privacy Policy and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
  • Confidentiality never overrides the duty to report under Section 14. No person may promise a child or family that a disclosure will be kept secret.
  • Safeguarding records are retained for as long as required by law and for a reasonable period thereafter.

18. Training and Awareness

  • Every new trustee, staff member and volunteer receives a briefing on this Policy before beginning work.
  • Personnel in roles involving contact with children receive refresher briefings at least annually.
  • This Policy is published on the Website and available on request in Tamil.
  • Key helpline numbers are displayed at the Trust's office and carried by field teams.

19. Breach and Disciplinary Action

Breach of this Policy is treated with the utmost seriousness. Depending on severity, the Trust may:

  • issue a formal warning;
  • remove the person from activities involving children;
  • terminate the volunteer engagement, employment or contract;
  • report the matter to the police and any relevant authority;
  • inform partner organisations where there is a continuing risk to children.

Any breach involving actual or suspected harm to a child will result in immediate removal and a report to the authorities. Concealing a concern, discouraging a report, or retaliating against a reporter is itself a serious breach warranting removal.
