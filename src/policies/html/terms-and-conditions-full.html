2. About the Trust

Nallathae Nadakkum Samuga Sevai Arakkattalai is an irrevocable public charitable trust registered under Deed No. 16/2025 dated 8 April 2025 with the Sub-Registrar, Tiruchengode Town & Taluk, Namakkal District, Tamil Nadu, India, and governed by a Board of Trustees.

Trust assets can never be redirected to any personal profit. Financial transactions require the joint signatures of the Chairman and the Treasurer; no single trustee may withdraw or dispense public donations without Board approval. Accounts are maintained as a complete ledger and audited annually by a certified Chartered Accountant.

Our work includes daily food distribution (Annadhanam), a free emergency ambulance service, last rites for the unclaimed, elderly rescue and rehousing, education support, and emergency medical fundraising, in and around Tiruchengode, Tamil Nadu.

7. Requesting Help

The Trust provides assistance according to need, eligibility and available resources. Submitting a request does not create an entitlement, a contract, or any obligation on the Trust to provide assistance.

  • Every request is physically verified by our local volunteer network before resources are allocated. This protects donors and ensures help reaches genuine cases.
  • You must provide accurate and truthful information and supporting documents where asked, such as hospital estimates, diagnosis records, academic records or fee demand sheets.
  • Assistance may be declined, deferred, reduced or withdrawn where information is found to be false or incomplete, where eligibility is not met, where resources are unavailable, or where the request falls outside our objects or geographic area.
  • Some services are triggered through official channels rather than public request - last rites for the unclaimed, for example, normally begin with communication from the police or hospital authorities.
  • Providing false information to obtain charitable assistance may amount to cheating under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 and diverts help from someone in genuine need.

We aim to respond to requests as quickly as our resources allow, but we give no guarantee of response time or outcome. Emergency cases are prioritised.

8. Emergency Ambulance Service

In a life-threatening emergency, call 108 (ambulance) or 112 (emergency response) first.

The Trust's ambulance is a charitable service with a single vehicle and limited coverage. It is not a substitute for the State emergency medical service and must not be relied on as your only means of obtaining emergency transport.

The following conditions apply to the ambulance service:

  • It is provided free of charge and is reserved strictly for individuals and families below the poverty line who cannot afford standard private ambulance charges. It is not a general-purpose or paid ambulance service.
  • Eligibility is verified at the time of dispatch, over the call.
  • Availability is subject to the vehicle being free at the time of the call. We cannot guarantee availability.
  • Transfers focus on Government Hospitals.
  • The vehicle is equipped with life-support essentials and GPS tracking. It is not an advanced life support (ALS) ambulance unless specifically stated, and staffing depends on volunteer availability.
  • The Trust and its volunteers act in good faith and are not liable for clinical outcomes, delays, traffic conditions, vehicle breakdown, hospital admission decisions, or any consequence of transport, save in the case of gross negligence or wilful misconduct.

Volunteers are the centre of our work, and we welcome anyone of any background. Volunteering is offered and accepted on the following basis:

  • Volunteering is unpaid and voluntary. It creates no employment relationship, no contract of service, and no entitlement to wages, stipend, benefits or reimbursement unless expressly agreed in writing.
  • You must be at least eighteen (18) years of age to volunteer independently. Volunteers under 18 may participate only with the written consent of a parent or lawful guardian and under supervision.
  • The Trust may accept, decline, limit or end any volunteer engagement at its discretion, without giving reasons.
  • Volunteers must comply with these Terms, our Child Protection Policy, and any briefing, code of conduct or safety instruction given to them.
  • Volunteers must not speak for the Trust, raise funds, make commitments to beneficiaries, or represent the Trust publicly without written authorisation.
  • Volunteers must respect the confidentiality and dignity of beneficiaries and must not disclose or publish their information, images or circumstances without consent.
  • Field work may involve physical activity, travel, exposure to weather, hospital and mortuary environments, and distressing situations. You participate at your own risk and should not undertake any activity beyond your physical or emotional capacity. Please tell us of any medical condition relevant to your safety.
  • The Trust does not currently provide volunteer insurance. You are responsible for your own health and accident cover.
  • Due to legal and sensitive requirements, volunteers assist with non-legal logistics only in last-rites work.

10. Blood Donation

Registration as a voluntary blood donor is a statement of willingness, not a binding commitment. You may decline any individual request and withdraw your registration at any time.

  • Blood is donated only at a licensed blood bank or hospital, subject to that institution's medical screening, eligibility criteria and procedures.
  • The Trust does not collect, store, test or transport blood. Our role is limited to connecting willing donors with requests.
  • The Trust is not liable for any medical consequence of donation, for the screening, handling or use of donated blood, or for the acts of any blood bank or hospital.
  • Blood donation is never bought or sold through the Trust. Any payment for blood is illegal and must be reported to us.

11. Material Donations

We accept clothing, groceries, educational materials and similar goods. Please contact us before sending, so we can confirm current requirements and avoid waste.

  • Clothing must be new or gently used - not heavily worn, torn or stained - so it can be given with dignity.
  • Food and groceries must be unopened, unexpired and fit for consumption.
  • The Trust may decline any material donation that is unsuitable, unsafe, unhygienic or not needed at the time.
  • Once accepted, goods become the property of the Trust and cannot be returned, exchanged or converted into money. Goods that cannot be used as intended may be redirected to another charitable purpose or responsibly disposed of.
  • Transport and delivery costs are borne by the donor unless separately agreed.

  • Consent may be withdrawn at any time. On request we will remove the material from our own website and social media channels within a reasonable period, though we cannot recall material already printed, downloaded, or shared onward by third parties.
